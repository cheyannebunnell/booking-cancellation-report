Where do cancellations come from, and what should we do about them?

36,285 hotel bookings. Every number below is computed from booking.csv; sections end with the decision they support.

1. The risk map: lead time × channel

Cancellation rate (cell color) and number of bookings. Cells with fewer than 30 bookings are left blank.

2. Deposit policy simulator

Flag every booking made at least T days ahead (for the chosen channel) and require a deposit or non-refundable rate. How many cancellations would that cover, and how many loyal guests would be bothered?

Precision is the share of flagged bookings that really cancel; recall is the share of all cancellations that get flagged. Raising T flags fewer guests with a higher cancel rate but leaves more cancellations uncovered.

3. The hidden hot spot: bookings with no special request

Cancellation rate by channel and number of special requests on the booking.

4. Overbooking planner

How many bookings can you accept for a fixed number of rooms before the chance of having more arrivals than rooms exceeds your tolerance? Assumes each booking cancels independently at the chosen rate.

The point of the planner: a corporate-heavy mix needs only a small cushion, while long-lead online bookings need a very large one. A single hotel-wide overbooking rule would be wrong for both.

5. Don't pool the calendar

Monthly bookings (bars) and cancellation rate (line), using the date given in the file, July 2017 onward.
